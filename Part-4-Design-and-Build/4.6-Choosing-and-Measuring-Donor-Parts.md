# Topic 4.6 - Choosing and Measuring Donor Parts

> **"A donor part saves engineering effort only when its interfaces are understood."**

---

# Learning Objectives 🎯

By the end of this topic you will be able to:

- write the donor job before comparing products or prices
- judge candidates using evidence, condition, interfaces and whole cost
- capture a safe unpowered baseline before dismantling
- disassemble with a recoverable photo, orientation and fastener record
- measure critical interfaces from shared datums with honest uncertainty
- prove risky dimensions using fit templates and minimum-size test coupons

---

# Before We Begin

The bargain donor buggy arrives looking complete.

Before anyone measures it, the screws are removed and tipped into one tray.
The gearbox seemed smooth before dismantling, but nobody recorded it.
One mounting face is copied beautifully into CAD, yet the driveshaft clips the new chassis through suspension travel.

The donor did not fail.
The evidence trail did.

Topic 4.5 proved the control architecture.
Now we replace household-material precision mechanisms with proven parts while keeping our own structure modular.

Use six checks:

1. **Job** - what exact function must the donor provide?
2. **Evidence** - which manual, dimensions and condition facts support the choice?
3. **Baseline** - what works before anything changes?
4. **Datum** - which shared references locate the irregular part?
5. **Interface** - where does it touch, move, fasten, connect and receive service?
6. **Prove** - can a cheap template or coupon confirm the risky fit?

# Buy Precision, Build Structure

Some parts demand accurate manufacturing, specialist materials or many years of development.

Useful donor parts may include:

- gearbox and differential
- driveshafts
- wheel hubs and wheel hexes
- steering knuckles
- suspension arms
- hinge pins
- bearings
- shock absorbers
- gears and shafts
- servo saver
- motor plate
- wheels and tyres

Parts worth designing around them include:

- chassis rails or plates
- adapter blocks
- servo mount
- battery tray
- electronics tray
- bumpers
- cable guides
- protective covers

The donor does not reduce learning.
It changes the learning from precision manufacture to systems integration.

# Write the Donor Job Before Searching

Start with functions, not a brand name.

Example donor requirement:

```text
Provide a serviceable rear 2WD drivetrain with differential, bearings,
output shafts, wheel interfaces and replaceable gears.
It must fit the controlled track and wheelbase range, accept the selected
motor or an approved adapter, and have identifiable spare parts.
```

Create requirements for:

- drive layout: 2WD or 4WD
- intended low-power first use
- target track range
- wheel interface
- motor type and mounting path
- steering or suspension module needs
- documentation
- spares availability
- maximum whole cost
- safe battery exclusion or handling plan

Do not buy a donor because it looks the right size in a photograph.

# Choose the Donor Scope

## Complete rolling donor

Provides geometry, matched parts, fasteners and evidence that systems once worked together.

Advantages:

- matched suspension and drivetrain
- known assembly order
- many small parts included
- easier baseline testing

Limitations:

- may contain worn or damaged hidden parts
- includes structure you may not reuse
- postage can be higher

## Donor module or parts lot

Provides only the chosen system.

Advantages:

- lower bulk
- fewer irrelevant parts
- may be new replacement parts

Limitations:

- missing fasteners, shafts or bearings
- uncertain compatibility between listings
- no working baseline

For a first build, a complete documented donor or complete new module is often safer than a mixed loose-parts bundle.

# Evidence Beats Listing Language

Words such as “universal”, “standard”, “1/10 scale” or “fits most” are not dimensions.

Useful evidence includes:

- official assembly manual
- official exploded diagram
- manufacturer part numbers
- dimensioned drawing
- clear photographs of the exact item
- working demonstration
- seller's measured dimensions with method stated
- availability of replacement gears and shafts

A model-scale label does not guarantee the same wheelbase, track, hole pattern or motor size.

# Calculate Whole Donor Cost

The listing price is not the project cost.

```text
whole donor cost = item + delivery + missing parts + service parts + adapters
```

## Worked Example

**Question:** Which donor is cheaper when missing parts are included?

**Candidate A:**

- rolling donor: £38
- delivery: £7
- replacement bearings: £8
- total: £53

**Candidate B:**

- loose rear module: £25
- delivery: £4
- missing driveshafts: £12
- missing wheel hubs: £10
- fasteners: £5
- total: £56

**Meaning:**

Candidate A costs £3 less in this example and provides a complete geometry baseline.
Candidate B's lower listing price hid missing interfaces.

Prices change, so use current evidence at the actual purchase gate.

# Inspect Before Power or Dismantling

If a used donor arrives, do not immediately connect an unknown battery.

With an adult, inspect:

- model identity and labels
- damaged, swollen or leaking batteries
- cut or melted wires
- seized drivetrain
- cracked suspension
- missing fasteners
- tyre deterioration
- rust or contamination
- unusual modification
- correct charger and manual availability

An unknown, damaged or swollen battery is not a project resource.
Isolate it safely and follow local disposal guidance with an adult.

# Baseline-Test the Donor

Before dismantling, capture what works.

With power disconnected:

- roll it by hand
- rotate wheels slowly
- feel gear smoothness
- inspect differential action
- move steering through its range
- compress suspension one corner at a time
- check left-right symmetry
- photograph ride height and linkage orientation

Powered testing is optional and only allowed when the complete electrical and battery system is identified, undamaged, compatible and adult-approved.

The unpowered baseline is still valuable.

> **[Figure 4.6.4, F4 sequence strip: adult-led donor intake shows identity and manual check, battery isolated, visual damage inspection, hand-rolling and steering/suspension baseline, then labelled pass/repair/unknown record. A red stop branch leads away from swollen, leaking, damaged or unidentified batteries. Caption: Inspection and an unpowered baseline come before dismantling or optional powered work. Alt text: Safe donor intake sequence isolates batteries and records mechanical condition before disassembly.]**

# Create a Controlled Disassembly Record

Taking something apart is easy.
Knowing how it returns is engineering.

Use:

- numbered photographs
- shallow parts tray
- labelled bags or folded paper packets
- fastener map
- orientation arrows
- manual or exploded diagram
- decision log

Remove one module at a time.

For every step, record:

```text
Step 07
Removed: rear shock tower
Fasteners: 4 × M3 × 12 button-head screws
Orientation: raised face forward
Washers/spacers: two 1 mm spacers behind tower
Photo: D07
Reason: access gearbox mounting face
```

Do not mix “nearly identical” screws.
A slightly longer screw can damage a gearbox or bind a rotating part.

> **[Figure 4.6.5, F1 annotated photograph: one disassembly stage arranged on a neutral mat with the donor module, numbered photo card D07, four screws placed in a matching outline, two spacers, FRONT arrow and labelled packet. Callouts link each item to one line in the record. Caption: A controlled stage preserves position, orientation and fastener identity together. Alt text: Numbered donor disassembly record connects a photograph, parts layout and labelled fastener packet.]**

# Select Interface Datums

Irregular parts need simple reference features.

Useful donor datums include:

- flat mounting face
- axle centreline
- gearbox centre plane
- motor shaft axis
- pair of widely spaced mounting holes
- bearing bore
- wheel hex face
- hinge-pin axis

Use the minimum datum set that locates the part fully.

For example:

```text
Primary datum A: flat gearbox mounting face
Secondary datum B: rear axle centreline
Tertiary datum C: chassis centre plane
```

Then measure every critical feature from A, B or C rather than chaining many small dimensions.

> **[Figure 4.6.6, F6 measured drawing: irregular gearbox in front, side and top views. Datum A is the flat mounting face, B the rear axle centreline and C the chassis centre plane; critical holes and shaft axes are dimensioned directly from them while a crossed-out chain of small dimensions runs around the cosmetic casing. Caption: Shared datums prevent small measurement errors from accumulating across an irregular part. Alt text: Gearbox features are located from three common datums instead of a long dimension chain.]**

# Measure What the Chassis Needs

Capture six interface groups.

## 1. Solid envelope

- maximum length
- maximum width
- maximum height
- protruding shafts and connectors

## 2. Mounting interface

- hole count
- hole diameter or fastener size
- centre-to-centre spacing
- counterbore or recess
- mounting-face shape

## 3. Motion interface

- axle or hinge-pin axes
- driveshaft sweep
- suspension travel
- steering movement
- rotating clearances

## 4. Power interface

- motor type
- shaft diameter and length
- mounting-hole pattern
- gear mesh adjustment direction
- connector/wire route

## 5. Wheel interface

- hex size or pin type
- axle thread
- hub face position
- wheel offset dependency

## 6. Service interface

- removal direction
- tool path
- gear-cover opening
- bearing replacement route
- lubrication or inspection access

Do not measure cosmetic logos, fillets or ribs unless they touch the new structure or limit movement.

> **[Figure 4.6.7, F5 motion and service overlay: the same donor module appears in six transparent layers—solid body, mounting contact, suspension and driveshaft sweep, motor and wire route, wheel interface, and tool/removal path. Red collision zones show why the static outline alone is insufficient. Caption: A part must fit while moving, connecting and leaving for service—not only while standing still. # Use the Right Measuring Method

| Feature | Basic method | Upgrade method |
|---|---|---|
| Overall envelope | Steel rule and square | Callipers |
| Hole spacing | Measure far edges and subtract one diameter | Callipers or pins |
| Hole pattern | Paper rubbing/template | Callipers and CAD sketch |
| Shaft diameter | Compare with known holes | Callipers/micrometer |
| Axis height | Rule, blocks and square | Height gauge setup |
| Movement envelope | Card pointer and tracing | Photos/video with scale |

Callipers improve resolution but do not remove judgement.
Soft plastic, chamfers and inaccessible centres can still create error.

# Measure Hole Spacing Carefully

The centre of a hole is difficult to locate with a ruler.

For two equal holes, measure from the left edge of the first to the left edge of the second.
That equals centre-to-centre spacing if the edges correspond.

Another method:

```text
centre distance = outside-to-outside distance - one hole diameter
```

## Worked Example

**Known values:**

- outside-to-outside distance = 46.2 mm
- equal hole diameter = 3.2 mm

**Calculation:**

```text
centre distance = 46.2 - 3.2
                = 43.0 mm
```

Measure three times, remove and replace the tool between readings, then record the spread.

> **[Figure 4.6.8, F6 measured drawing: two equal Ø3.2 holes show three valid centre-spacing methods—corresponding left edges, outside span minus one diameter, and pins with callipers. A small inset shows the error caused by guessing each centre with a ruler. Caption: Measure accessible edges that preserve the centre distance instead of estimating invisible centres. Alt text: Equal-hole spacing is derived from corresponding edges or an outside span and hole diameter.]**

# Record Measurement Uncertainty

Do not turn `about 43 mm` into `43.000 mm` in CAD.

Use a measurement table:

| Feature | Reading 1 | Reading 2 | Reading 3 | Recorded value | Confidence |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Mount spacing | 43.0 | 43.1 | 43.0 | 43.0 mm | High |
| Shaft height | 18 | 19 | 18 | 18.5 mm provisional | Low |

Large spread means the method or access needs improvement.

Remember accuracy versus precision from Topic 1.6.
Three matching readings can still be wrong if the wrong feature was measured.

> **🤔 Think about it.** Three people measure the same cosmetic rib and obtain exactly 24.0 mm. Does their agreement prove the motor will align?

No. The readings may be precise, but the chosen feature does not control the motor interface. Reliable measurement requires the correct datum and feature as well as repeatable technique.

# Make Templates Before CAD

A paper or card template can test a hole pattern immediately.

Methods include:

- pencil rubbing over a flat face
- tracing with a square reference edge
- pressing safe screw tips or removable pins through card with adult help
- photocopying or scanning a flat part beside a scale, if the scanner can be used without damage

Label the template `TOP`, `FRONT`, datum and scale.

Offer it to the donor part without forcing fasteners.

This is a **fit template**, a cheap physical check of an interface.

# Build Test Coupons for Risky Fits

Topic 2.5 introduced test coupons.

Before printing a whole chassis in Topic 4.7, plan small coupons for:

- bearing seat
- screw clearance
- captured nut
- servo pocket
- motor-hole pattern
- donor mounting pattern
- hinge-pin hole

Each coupon should answer one fit question using little material.

> **☕ Good place to pause.**
>
> Finish the donor inventory, photographs and datum plan before entering measurements.
> A neat spreadsheet cannot rescue an unidentified feature.

# Hands-On Activity 1 - Critical or Cosmetic?

Choose one household mechanism such as a stapler or door hinge.

List ten visible dimensions.
Mark only those needed to mount it and preserve its motion.

Explain why the others can remain unmodelled.

# Hands-On Activity 2 - Measure, Remove and Prove

Measure the same part three times, fully removing the ruler or callipers each time.

Compare readings with a second person.
Record whether disagreement comes from tool resolution, datum choice or technique.

Then create a paper hole-pattern template of the same harmless, disconnected part.

Mark datum edges and hole centres.
Offer it back to the part and record any mismatch.

# Hands-On Activity 3 - Exploded-View Reconstruction

Lay out five safe non-powered parts in removal order.

Ask another person to reconstruct the order using only your numbered photograph and fastener map.

# Topic Project - Donor Interface Atlas 🛠️

Create a keepable measured record that another person could use to design the new chassis interfaces.

## Materials

- approved donor or representative safe module
- official manual and exploded diagram where available
- camera
- ruler and square
- callipers as an optional upgrade
- paper and card
- pencil and fine marker
- labelled trays, bags or envelopes
- existing fastener tools from Topic 2.7
- Build Passport

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Show a responsible adult or guardian the donor, disassembly plan, tools and battery condition before starting, and work with them nearby.
> Remove and isolate all power before inspection, measurement or disassembly.
> Do not use, charge or dismantle damaged, swollen, leaking, unknown or modified batteries; an adult must arrange safe handling and local disposal.
> Springs, clips and loaded suspension parts can release suddenly; follow the exact manual and use eye protection where the adult risk assessment requires it.
> Use the correct driver, keep tools away from electronics and never force a seized fastener.
> Cover sharp shafts and keep every small part away from young children and pets.

> **🎬 Watch the build**
>
> - Your donor manufacturer: search the exact model plus "official assembly manual exploded view" before removing any part.
> - Tamiya official support: search the exact kit number plus "manual" for examples of numbered fasteners and staged RC assembly.
> - Onshape Learning Centre: search "measure and reverse engineer a part" for the later CAD translation.

## Step 1 - Approve the donor job

Attach the requirement and decision matrix to the Atlas.

## Step 2 - Record identity and evidence

List model, part numbers, manuals, condition and purchase source.

## Step 3 - Complete the baseline

Photograph all sides with a ruler in view.

Record unpowered rolling, steering, suspension and drivetrain behaviour.

## Step 4 - Inventory modules

List reusable precision systems, reusable hardware, structure not reused and damaged items.

## Step 5 - Plan datums

Draw A, B and C datums on photographs or sketches.

## Step 6 - Disassemble only as far as needed

Photograph each step and map every fastener.

## Step 7 - Measure solid envelopes

Use top, side and front sketches.

## Step 8 - Measure mounting interfaces

Take three readings for critical patterns.

## Step 9 - Trace motion and service envelopes

Move parts by hand with power disconnected.

## Step 10 - Record power and wheel interfaces

Include shaft axes, motor pattern, gear adjustment and wheel hex or hub face.

## Step 11 - Make physical templates

Create card patterns for every chassis-facing donor interface.

## Step 12 - Validate

Ask another person to place templates and identify datum orientation without verbal help.

## Step 13 - Define coupons

List each risky fit and the smallest printed test that can answer it.

## Step 14 - Reassemble or store safely

Either restore the donor using the manual or keep modules labelled and protected.

## Step 15 - Reflection

Record:

- which precision system saved the most effort
- the measurement with lowest confidence
- one hidden whole-cost item discovered
- the first coupon Topic 4.7 must print

Keep the Atlas with the donor parts.
It is the bridge between a proven mechanism and your own chassis.

# Learning Goal

Select proven precision systems and capture trustworthy, testable interfaces without wasting effort modelling irrelevant detail.

# Cheapest Valid Prototype

Research and measure one safe donor module already owned before buying a complete platform.

A full donor is justified when it provides matched geometry, documentation and missing small hardware more economically.

# What to Buy, Reuse and Make

## Buy now

Only the donor or module that passes the written gate.

Possible service parts are purchased only after inspection confirms the need.

## Reuse now

- drivetrain, differential and gearbox
- hubs, shafts, bearings and wheel hexes
- steering knuckles, arms and hinge pins
- shocks and springs
- fasteners and motor plate
- wheels and tyres where suitable

## Make now

- decision matrix
- condition and baseline record
- fastener map
- datum drawings
- interface measurement tables
- card fit templates
- coupon plan
- Donor Interface Atlas

## Buy later

Do not buy a large filament supply or print the full chassis before interface coupons pass.

# Cost Checkpoint

Donor cost is project-specific.
Use a gate such as:

```text
maximum approved donor total = item + delivery + essential service parts + missing interfaces
```

Record actual spending separately for:

- donor purchase
- delivery
- replacement bearings/gears
- missing fasteners or shafts
- cleaning materials
- adapters
- unusable items

Compare the total with the cost of complete new modules and the learning value retained.

# Test Plan

## Test 1 - Donor Decision Gate

**Question:** Does the selected donor meet every mandatory function and evidence requirement?

**Independent variable:** Donor candidate.

**Dependent variable:** Requirements passed, failed or unknown.

**Control variables:** Same requirement set, weights, budget boundary and evidence rules.

**Procedure:** Evaluate each candidate and refuse points for unsupported claims.

**Pass condition:** The selected donor passes every mandatory requirement; unknown critical interfaces block purchase.

## Test 2 - Baseline and Recovery

**Question:** Which donor mechanisms work before dismantling?

**Independent variable:** Mechanism tested.

**Dependent variable:** Smoothness, symmetry, free movement and visible damage.

**Control variables:** Power-disconnected condition, inspection method and load.

**Procedure:** Roll, rotate, steer and compress systematically; record video or observation.

**Independent variable:** Module or disassembly stage checked.

**Dependent variables:** Smoothness, symmetry, correct reconstruction, fastener placement and unexplained parts.

**Control variables:** Same power-disconnected condition, manual, tools, inspection method and record.

**Procedure:** Record the unpowered mechanisms before change, then ask another person to explain or safely reconstruct one stage from the evidence.

**Pass condition:** Every reused mechanism has known working or repair status, and the record restores the stage without wrong screws, missing orientation or unexplained parts.

## Test 3 - Interface Measurement and Proof

**Question:** Can another person reproduce a critical interface from the Atlas?

**Independent variable:** Measurement or template revision.

**Dependent variables:** Reading spread, hole alignment, face contact and orientation clarity.

**Control variables:** Same donor part, datums, tool, method and gentle fitting rule.

**Procedure:** Take three independent readings, ask a second person to repeat the critical values, then create and offer a card template without force.

**Pass condition:** The reading spread stays inside the chosen limit, the template seats in only the intended orientation and all critical holes align visibly.

# Upgrade Path

Card templates -> dimensioned interface sketches -> one-feature test coupons -> small adapter blocks -> modular printed chassis -> revised donor adapters for alternative platforms.

# Stop Point Before Topic 4.7

Do not print the modular chassis until:

- donor requirements and whole cost are approved
- the donor identity and documentation are recorded
- battery and electrical hazards are isolated
- the unpowered baseline is complete
- reusable and damaged parts are separated
- disassembly photographs and fastener maps are complete
- A, B and C datums are defined
- critical solid, mounting, motion, power, wheel and service interfaces are measured
- critical readings are repeated
- low-confidence dimensions are visibly marked
- card fit templates pass
- risky fits have coupon plans
- donor modules are reassembled or safely labelled and stored
- the Build Passport and cost ledger are updated

The next material commitment is a few small coupons, not a full chassis print.

# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌

## Mistake 1 - Shopping Before Writing the Donor Job

Function and interfaces must drive the choice.

> **[Figure 4.6.11, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: left panel shows a child buying a shiny donor from a scale label while requirement boxes remain blank; right panel begins with the written drivetrain job and rejects a candidate missing a mandatory interface. Caption: A donor is selected to perform a defined job, not to match an attractive photograph. Alt text: Impulse donor shopping is contrasted with requirement-led selection.]**

## Mistake 2 - Treating Scale Labels as Dimensions

Measure wheelbase, track, envelopes and patterns directly.

> **[Figure 4.6.12, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: two products both labelled 1/10 have visibly different track, wheelbase and mounting patterns; the correct panel replaces the shared label with measured values and official drawings. Caption: A scale category does not define the interfaces the chassis must fit. Alt text: Same-scale donor products differ until direct dimensions expose the mismatch.]**

## Mistake 3 - Dismantling Before the Baseline

You lose evidence of original function and orientation.

> **[Figure 4.6.13, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: left panel shows an opened gearbox and mixed parts beside unanswered “Was it smooth?” and “Which way forward?” notes; right panel shows rolling, steering and photo evidence completed before the first screw is removed. Caption: A baseline preserves the facts that disassembly destroys. Alt text: Unrecorded dismantling is contrasted with a completed unpowered baseline.]**

## Mistake 4 - Mixing Fasteners and Orientation

Small length differences can damage or bind mechanisms.

> **[Figure 4.6.14, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: left panel shows nearly identical screws mixed in one cup and a long screw entering a gearbox; right panel uses numbered packets, a fastener map, spacers and FRONT arrows. Caption: Tiny hardware differences become controlled when each removal stage keeps its identity. Alt text: Mixed donor screws are contrasted with labelled packets and orientation records.]**

## Mistake 5 - Measuring the Wrong Geometry Precisely

Capture only geometry that affects fit, motion, force or service.

Measure critical features from shared datums and keep the digits honest.

> **[Figure 4.6.15, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: left panel crowds an irregular casing with three-decimal dimensions chained around logos and ribs; right panel dimensions only contact faces, axes, holes, envelopes and tool paths from A, B and C. Caption: Precision is useful only when it describes the geometry the new structure needs. Alt text: Excessive cosmetic measurements are contrasted with a concise datum-based interface drawing.]**

## Mistake 6 - Printing the Whole Interface First

Use card templates and test coupons.

Unknown or damaged donor batteries are excluded rather than treated as savings.

> **[Figure 4.6.16, F3 right-versus-wrong pair: left panel shows a full chassis print beside an untested hole pattern and an unknown battery marked “free”; right panel isolates the battery and tests the pattern with card plus a tiny coupon. Caption: The smallest safe proof should carry uncertainty before the full build does. Alt text: Premature full printing and unsafe battery reuse are contrasted with isolation, templates and coupons.]**

# Topic Summary 📝

- Donor precision systems let the learner focus on integration and structure.
- Write the donor's function before searching products.
- Complete donors offer matched geometry; loose parts may hide missing interfaces.
- Official manuals, exploded views and part numbers are stronger than vague listing language.
- Whole cost includes delivery, missing parts, service parts and adapters.
- Inspect and baseline-test before dismantling.
- Controlled disassembly preserves fastener, orientation and function evidence.
- Datums simplify measurements of irregular parts.
- Measure solid, mounting, motion, power, wheel and service interfaces.
- Critical readings need repetition and honest uncertainty.
- Card templates test patterns before CAD.
- Small coupons test risky printed fits before the chassis.
- The Donor Interface Atlas controls Topic 4.7.
