# REF-002 — Recommendation Report: In-Topic Illustration Strategy

**Date:** 2026-08-06
**Prepared for:** VoltForge Gear — The Young Engineer's Handbook
**Question:** The visual content inside topics is currently almost entirely
Mermaid diagrams. For an engineering book that must pull an 11-year-old's
curiosity, what should the in-topic figures actually be, how many, and how do
we produce them?
**Scope:** Figures *within* topic text only. Topic covers, the animated host
and video work are out of scope and unaffected.
**Status:** Recommendation for decision. No work started.
**Companion:** [REF-001](ref-001.md) concluded that illustrations, not the
publishing toolchain, are the real blocker. This report is that argument
worked out in detail.

---

## Executive Summary

**The book is 5–10× under-illustrated, and the shortfall is worst exactly
where the content is most physical.**

| Part | Lines | Figures | Mermaid | Lines per figure |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 — Engineering Mindset | 15,393 | 39 | 54 | 394 |
| 2 — Workshop Skills | 14,049 | 26 | 15 | 540 |
| 3 — Electronics and Vehicle Systems | 8,263 | 11 | 4 | 751 |
| **Total** | **37,705** | **76** | **73** | **496** |

A well-illustrated technical book for this age runs roughly one figure per one
to two pages. At about 40 lines per page here, that is one figure per 40–80
lines. We are at one per 496.

Three findings behind the headline:

1. **Mermaid has become the default visual, and it is the wrong default.**
   73 Mermaid blocks against 76 figure placeholders means roughly half the
   book's visual content is boxes-and-arrows. Mermaid draws *relationships*.
   Engineering needs *things*: cross-sections, exploded views, force arrows on
   a real shape, a hand holding a tool at the correct angle. The style guide
   already says this (section 7: never Mermaid for spatial or physical ideas)
   — practice has drifted from policy.
2. **Part 3 is the least illustrated part and should be the most.** Nine of
   its ten topics contain *exactly one* placeholder, which reads as a template
   minimum being satisfied rather than the content being served. This is the
   part covering Ackermann geometry, gear mesh, commutation, wire
   cross-sections and suspension travel — all irreducibly visual.
3. **89% of the existing placeholders are accuracy-critical** (68 of 76). This
   is not decorative work, and it constrains how the figures can be made.

**Recommendation:** adopt a figure taxonomy and a per-topic budget, retire
Mermaid from spatial duty, and produce figures in three deterministic streams —
annotated photographs, vector line drawings in a consistent hand-drawn style,
and CAD renders reused from the book's own models. Target ~340 figures.
Prioritise Part 3, then the Part 2 tool topics.

---

## 1. Why Mermaid cannot carry this book

Mermaid is excellent at what it is for: flows, cycles, decision trees, system
trees. The handbook has genuine uses for it — the Pause/Plan/Protect routine,
the engineering design cycle, the input-process-output chain.

It fails at everything else this book teaches, because it can only draw boxes
joined by lines. It cannot show:

- what a cross-threaded screw looks like versus a correctly started one
- the shaded kerf sitting on the waste side of a cut line
- why the inside wheel must turn more sharply in a corner
- solder wetting a joint versus balling up on top of it
- how layer lines run through a bracket and where it will therefore snap

A worked example from this repository: Topic 2.1 drew its four-zone workspace —
a *spatial layout of a table* — as a Mermaid flowchart, with a sketch
placeholder for the same idea directly beneath it. The review pass deleted the
Mermaid and promoted the sketch. **That pattern is likely repeated across the
54 Mermaid blocks in Part 1**, and auditing them is a concrete, bounded task.

> **Rule of thumb to adopt:** if the reader needs to know what something
> *looks like*, where it *is*, or which way a force *points*, it is not a
> Mermaid diagram.

## 2. A figure taxonomy for this book

Seven types cover essentially everything the topics need. Naming them matters:
it lets a topic's figure list be specified before anyone draws anything, and it
keeps 340 figures looking like one book.

| # | Type | What it does | Typical use | Production |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **F1** | **Annotated photograph** | Shows a real object with labels | Tools, fasteners, filament, a finished joint, the actual buggy | Photo + vector labels |
| **F2** | **Cross-section** | Cuts a part open to show what cannot be seen | Fits, countersinks, heat-set inserts, layer structure, a solder joint | Vector |
| **F3** | **Right-versus-wrong pair** | Two panels, correct beside incorrect | Every "Common Beginner Mistakes" section | Vector or paired photo |
| **F4** | **Sequence strip** | 3–5 panels showing a process over time | Drilling a clean hole, making a joint, starting a screw | Vector |
| **F5** | **Force / motion overlay** | Arrows on a real shape showing what pushes where | Load paths, torque, weight transfer, steering geometry | Vector over photo or render |
| **F6** | **Measured drawing** | Dimensioned, to scale | Part briefs, coupons, hole patterns, anything the reader will build | CAD export |
| **F7** | **Comparison array** | 3+ variants side by side | Material behaviour, gear ratios, infill percentages, tyre types | Photo grid or vector |

Mermaid remains legitimate for flows and cycles only — call it **F0** and cap
it at roughly two per topic.

**The highest-value type for this book is F3, right-versus-wrong.** Every
topic already ends with `# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌`, currently pure prose.
An 11-year-old learns "that screw is cross-threaded" from seeing the two
pictures side by side far faster than from a paragraph — and it is the single
cheapest way to make the book feel like an engineering manual rather than a
textbook.

## 3. Where figures earn their place

The topic template already has natural anchors. Rather than inventing a rule,
attach figures to structure that exists:

| Existing template element | Figure duty |
| --- | --- |
| Signature visual | 1 per topic, the hardest idea, the most designed figure in the topic |
| `> **🤔 Think about it.**` | 1 figure showing the *setup* — the reader must be able to see what to try |
| `# Topic Mini Project 🛠️` | Sequence strip (F4) of the build, plus a photo of the finished artifact |
| `# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌` | One F3 pair per mistake, or one combined array |
| `# New Words 📖` | Any word naming a physical thing gets a thumbnail beside it |
| Hands-On Activities | The setup photo, minimum |
| Worked examples | The geometry the arithmetic refers to |

### Proposed per-topic budget

Replaces the current de-facto minimum of one.

| Topic type | Figures | Rationale |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Concept topics (most of Part 1) | 6–8 | Abstract ideas need the most concrete anchoring |
| Skill topics (Part 2) | 10–14 | Physical technique; the reader is copying what they see |
| Component topics (Part 3) | 10–12 | Every topic is a real object with real geometry |
| Build topics (Part 4) | 12–16 | A build step without a picture is a support request |
| Capstones / challenges | 4–6 | Mostly the reader's own artifacts |

Across 44 topics this lands at roughly **340 figures**. That is a real
programme of work, and it is what the book needs to be what it claims to be.

## 4. Production: three deterministic streams

REF-001 and the video feasibility study both landed on the same constraint,
and it holds here: **a beautiful but wrong diagram is worse than no diagram,
because a young reader cannot tell.** Every stream below is deterministic —
a human decides what the figure says.

### Stream A — Annotated photographs (cheapest, highest authenticity)

You own the objects. Screws, hex keys, filament, a printed bracket, a
soldering iron, a stripped wire, a good joint beside a cold one.

- Set up once: plain background, one consistent light, fixed camera height,
  a small scale reference in frame.
- Shoot a topic's worth in a single session.
- Label in vector afterwards so text stays crisp and translatable.
- **Best for:** all of Part 2's tools and fasteners, Part 3's components,
  every mini-project artifact.
- **This is where to start.** It needs no illustrator and no new skill, and it
  covers perhaps 40% of the total figure count.

### Stream B — Vector line drawings, consistent hand-drawn style

For everything that cannot be photographed because it is inside, invisible or
idealised: cross-sections, force arrows, geometry, right-versus-wrong pairs.

- **Recommend a deliberately hand-drawn look** (Excalidraw-style or similar)
  rather than crisp CAD-style linework. For an 11-year-old this reads as
  *approachable and sketchable* — it invites the reader to draw their own,
  which is exactly the behaviour the book wants. It also ages better than a
  half-polished technical style, and it forgives imperfection.
- Store as **SVG in the repo**, versioned alongside the topic that uses them,
  editable forever, and scaling cleanly to print.
- Build a small shared component library first: the buggy silhouette, a hand,
  a screw, an arrow set, the standard callout label. Consistency comes from
  reuse, not from discipline.

### Stream C — CAD renders (free, already being produced)

Topics 2.4 and 2.5 have the reader model real parts, and Part 4 builds the
buggy. Those models are dimensionally correct by construction.

- **Best for:** F6 measured drawings and any part anatomy.
- Effectively free: the models are being made anyway.
- Also solves a subtle problem — the figure and the thing the reader prints
  cannot disagree, because they come from the same file.

### What to avoid

Generated imagery for anything accuracy-critical, per the standing decision of
2026-07-21. That leaves it available for mood and story, which in-topic figures
mostly are not — 68 of the current 76 placeholders are accuracy-critical.

## 5. Conventions to add to STYLE-GUIDE.md section 7

The current policy names the sketch-placeholder format but not the figure
system. Proposed additions:

1. **Figure numbering.** `Figure N.M.k`, so prose can say "see Figure 3.9.2"
   instead of "the sketch above". None of the 76 placeholders is numbered
   today, which makes them unreferenceable.
2. **Captions.** Every figure gets one sentence saying what to notice — not a
   restatement of the title. The caption is where the teaching lands.
3. **Type tag in the placeholder**, e.g. `**[F2 cross-section: ...]**`, so the
   figure list can be filtered and costed per stream.
4. **Palette.** Reuse the established volt-blue / forge-orange / brushed-steel
   identity so in-topic figures match the covers. One accent colour per figure
   carries meaning (forge-orange = "look here" / danger / the thing that
   moves); everything else stays neutral.
5. **Labels are British English, sentence case, and never inside the drawing
   as rasterised text** — keep them as text elements so they can be corrected.
6. **Every figure must survive greyscale**, for print and for photocopies.
   Never encode meaning in colour alone.
7. **Accessibility:** alt text on every figure. It is also the fastest way to
   check a figure has a point — if the alt text is hard to write, the figure is
   unclear.

## 6. Priority order

Sequenced by shortfall against need, not by part number.

| Priority | Scope | Figures | Why first |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| **1** | **Part 3 (10 topics)** | ~110 | Worst density in the book (1 per 751 lines) and the most irreducibly visual content. Also the newest text, so figures can be specified during its review pass rather than retrofitted. |
| **2** | **Part 2 tool topics 2.7, 2.8, 2.9** | ~40 | Physical technique the reader copies with sharp and hot tools. The safety argument is strongest here, and Stream A photography covers most of it cheaply. |
| **3** | **Mermaid audit, Part 1** | — | Bounded review of 54 blocks; reclassify each as keep (F0), convert to a figure type, or delete. Cheap, and it removes visual clutter that is actively misleading. |
| **4** | **Part 1 concept topics** | ~50 | Already the best-illustrated part; improvement is real but marginal. |
| **5** | **Part 4/5 as written** | ~140 | Specify figures during authoring — far cheaper than retrofitting, which is the lesson Parts 1–3 are teaching us. |

**Standing rule going forward:** no new topic reaches v0.1 without its figure
list specified. Retrofitting 76 placeholders across 37,705 written lines is the
avoidable cost this report exists to document.

## 7. Risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 340 figures never get made and the plan stalls | High | Start with Stream A photography, which needs no illustrator. Ship per topic, not per part — a topic with 10 figures is finished and visible. |
| Figures drift in style across a long programme | High | Build the shared component library and palette rules before volume production, not after. |
| A figure contradicts the text after a review pass edits the prose | Medium | Figure lives next to its topic in the repo; treat "figures still match" as a checklist item in the per-topic review loop. |
| Accuracy errors in technical figures | Medium | Streams B and C are human-authored and CAD-derived; add figure review to the same research pass that verifies the topic's facts. |
| Photography quality is inconsistent session to session | Medium | Fixed setup written down once — background, light, height, scale reference. |
| Scope crowds out finishing the writing | Medium | Priority 1 attaches to Part 3's review pass, which is happening anyway. Nothing here requires a separate project until Parts 4–5. |

---

## 8. Recommendation in one paragraph

Adopt the seven-type figure taxonomy and the per-topic budget; add figure
numbering, captions, palette and greyscale rules to STYLE-GUIDE.md section 7;
produce figures in three deterministic streams with annotated photography
first; and start with Part 3, whose review pass has not yet begun and which is
both the least illustrated and the most visual part of the book. Treat "figures
specified" as part of the definition of done for every future topic, so this
report never has to be written again for Parts 4 and 5.
