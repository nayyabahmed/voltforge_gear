# Topic 3.8 - Gears and the Drivetrain

> **"The motor makes rotation; the drivetrain delivers the right speed and torque to the wheels."**

# Learning Objectives 🎯

By the end of this topic you will be able to identify drivetrain parts, calculate ratios, predict output direction, diagnose losses and build a geared model.

# Before We Begin

Ride a bicycle in a high gear up a steep hill and each pedal push feels difficult.
Select a lower gear and the wheel turns fewer times for each pedal turn, but climbing becomes easier.

The VoltForge buggy uses the same trade-off.
Its fast motor needs reduction gearing so the tyres receive useful torque.

# Follow the Force Path

The **drivetrain** is the complete mechanical route from motor to driven wheels.

```text
motor -> pinion -> spur gear -> gearbox/differential -> driveshafts -> hubs -> tyres
```

The exact arrangement varies between two-wheel-drive and four-wheel-drive buggies.

> **[Signature visual: motor pinion driving a larger spur gear. Tooth counts, rotation arrows, speed decrease and torque increase are marked. A second panel traces output through differential and two driveshafts.]**

# Pinion and Spur Gear

The small gear on the motor is usually the **pinion**.
The larger driven gear is commonly the **spur gear**.

Meshing external gears rotate in opposite directions.

Gear teeth must share compatible pitch or module and suitable tooth form.
Similar-looking gears do not necessarily mesh correctly.

# Gear Ratio

For one pinion driving one spur gear:

```text
reduction ratio = driven teeth / driving teeth
```

## Worked Example

A `20-tooth` pinion drives a `60-tooth` spur.

```text
ratio = 60 / 20
ratio = 3
```

This is a `3:1` reduction.
Ignoring losses, the spur turns once for every three pinion turns and receives about three times the torque.

If motor speed is `18,000 RPM`:

```text
spur speed = 18,000 / 3
spur speed = 6,000 RPM
```

Real output is lower because of load and losses.

# Compound and Internal Ratios

A gearbox or differential assembly may add another reduction.

```text
total ratio = first ratio x second ratio x ...
```

If the pinion/spur ratio is `3:1` and the internal ratio is `2.6:1`:

```text
total ratio = 3 x 2.6 = 7.8:1
```

Record whether a manufacturer quotes only internal ratio or total ratio.

# Ratio Trade-Off

More reduction generally gives:

- lower theoretical wheel speed
- greater wheel torque
- easier acceleration
- lower motor load for the same vehicle demand

Less reduction generally gives:

- higher theoretical wheel speed
- lower torque multiplication
- greater motor load
- more heat risk

`Gear up` and `gear down` can confuse beginners.
Always state actual pinion, spur and total ratio.

# Gear Mesh

Gear teeth need clearance for lubricant, errors and expansion.
Too tight causes friction, noise and heat.
Too loose causes impact, backlash and stripped teeth.

Use the manufacturer's mesh method.
A common paper-strip method exists for some hobby gears, but tooth size and instructions vary.

The motor mount must hold centre distance under load.

# Shafts, Bearings and Alignment

A shaft carries rotation and torque.
A **bearing** supports a rotating shaft while reducing friction and controlling position.

Misaligned bearings can bind even when each bearing is good.

Check:

- shaft straightness
- bearing fit
- axial movement
- gear location
- housing stiffness
- dirt and lubrication requirements

# Differential

When a buggy turns, the outside wheel travels farther than the inside wheel.
A **differential** lets the driven wheels rotate at different speeds while receiving torque.

Locking both wheels together can improve traction in some conditions but causes scrub in turns.

Differentials are precision specialist parts.
Use a proven donor or purchased unit and learn to mount, service and test it.

# Driveshafts and Joints

Driveshafts carry torque from differential outputs to wheel hubs.
Their joints must move through suspension and steering travel.

Check plunge, joint angle, retention and clearance at full suspension movement.
A shaft that fits at ride height may bind at full droop.

# Efficiency and Losses

Every mesh, bearing, joint and tyre deformation consumes some power.

Losses become:

- heat
- noise
- vibration
- material wear

Spin the drivetrain unpowered and feel for consistent resistance.
Sudden tight spots are evidence.

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Disconnect and remove the battery before touching gears or shafts.
> Use a gear cover during powered operation.
> Keep fingers, hair, clothing and tools away from rotating parts.
> Never stop a powered drivetrain by hand.

# Hands-On Activity 1 - Paper Gear Ratios

Draw or cut circles with `12`, `24` and `36` marked teeth divisions.
Pair them and calculate ratios before rotating the paper models.

# Hands-On Activity 2 - Direction Chain

Use three paper gears in a row.
Predict the final direction, then test it.
Notice that an idler changes direction but not the ideal ratio when its tooth count cancels.

# Hands-On Activity 3 - Differential Walk

Two people represent left and right wheels walking around a centre person.
The outside person must travel farther in the same turning time.
Explain why equal wheel speed would force slip.

# Hands-On Activity 4 - Friction Audit

Rotate a pencil in a tight card hole, then in a rolled-paper bearing.
Compare smoothness using the same shaft, load and turning method.

# Topic Mini Project - Cardboard Reduction Drivetrain 🛠️

Build two meshing corrugated-card gears on paper shafts, then test ratio, direction, mesh and load.

## Materials

- corrugated card, paper and two pencils or rolled-paper shafts
- ruler, compass or round objects, pens and scissors
- tape, glue, string and two bottle-cap spacers if available

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Show a responsible adult what you plan to build and build with them nearby.
> Use age-appropriate scissors and blunt shafts.
> Do not motorise the model.

> **🎬 Watch the build**
>
> - Science Buddies: search "cardboard gears" for visible construction examples.
> - BBC Bitesize KS3 Design and Technology: search "gears mechanical systems".

## Build steps

1. Cut one small and one large corrugated-card gear.
2. Mark and count teeth before cutting them consistently.
3. Mount each on a removable paper or pencil shaft.
4. Build slotted bearing supports so centre distance can adjust.
5. Mesh the gears with small visible clearance.
6. Mark one tooth on each gear.
7. Count input turns for one output turn.
8. Compare observed and calculated ratio.
9. Add rotation arrows and torque/speed labels.
10. Hang a very light paper-cup load from the output using string.
11. Compare small-driving-large with large-driving-small.
12. Add one idler and test direction.
13. Create fault cards for tight mesh, loose mesh, bent shaft, loose gear and misaligned supports.
14. Diagnose each fault from sound, resistance and movement.
15. Reflect on why card teeth cannot represent high-speed RC precision.
16. Keep the model for your showcase shelf.

# Cheapest Valid Prototype

The unpowered card gears test ratio, direction, mesh and alignment for `£0`.

# What to Buy, Reuse and Make

Reuse proven donor gears, differential, shafts and bearings when available.
Make motor mounts, gear-cover prototypes and bearing-seat coupons.
Buy precision gears, bearings and differential parts rather than printing high-speed tooth forms early.

# Modular Interfaces

Record motor shaft, pinion pitch, spur location, internal ratio, bearing sizes, output shafts, driveshaft length, hub interface, gear-cover envelope and service access.

# Test Plan

## Test 1 - Ratio

**Question:** Does measured ratio match tooth-count prediction?

**Independent variable:** Gear pair.

**Dependent variable:** Output turns per input turns.

**Controls:** Same model and counting method.

**Procedure:** Test three pairs over ten input turns.

**Pass condition:** Direction and ratio match within one card-tooth counting error.

## Test 2 - Mesh

**Question:** Which centre position turns smoothly without tooth skipping?

**Independent variable:** Centre distance.

**Dependent variable:** skips and turning resistance.

**Controls:** Same gears, shafts and load.

**Procedure:** Test tight, correct and loose positions.

**Pass condition:** No binding or skips through ten turns.

## Test 3 - Alignment

**Question:** Do supports keep shafts parallel?

**Independent variable:** Support position.

**Dependent variable:** visible wobble and resistance.

**Controls:** Same gears and mesh.

**Procedure:** Measure spacing at both shaft ends and rotate.

**Pass condition:** Spacing is equal within model tolerance and no tight spot appears.

# Upgrade Path

Card gears -> donor drivetrain measurement -> unpowered rolling assembly -> covered low-power test -> temperature/noise check -> evidence-based ratio change.

# Stop Point Before the Next Purchase

Stop until pitch/module, tooth counts, total ratio, shaft sizes, bearings, differential, wheel interfaces, cover and service plan are known.

# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌

## Mistake 1 - Calling a larger ratio faster

Define input, output and whether the number is a reduction.

## Mistake 2 - Mixing incompatible tooth pitches

Matching diameter does not make tooth systems compatible.

## Mistake 3 - Setting mesh with no clearance

Excessively tight mesh creates friction, noise and heat.

## Mistake 4 - Leaving mesh loose enough to skip

Excessive clearance produces impact and damaged teeth.

## Mistake 5 - Ignoring internal gearbox ratio

Multiply every reduction stage to obtain the total ratio.

## Mistake 6 - Misaligning bearings

Two good bearings can still bind when their axes do not agree.

## Mistake 7 - Testing powered gears without a cover

Guards and safe clearance are required around rotating gears.

## Mistake 8 - Printing precision gears too early

Prove interfaces and use suitable proven drivetrain parts first.

# Topic Summary 📝

- The drivetrain carries motor torque to the driven tyres.
- Reduction trades output speed for torque.
- Simple ratio equals driven teeth divided by driving teeth.
- Compound ratios multiply.
- Correct mesh needs compatible teeth, alignment and clearance.
- Bearings support shafts and reduce friction.
- A differential permits different wheel speeds in a turn.
- Driveshaft joints must work through suspension travel.
- Noise, heat and tight spots reveal losses.

# New Words 📖

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Backlash | Clearance that produces lost motion between gear teeth |
| Bearing | A component that supports a moving shaft and reduces friction |
| Differential | A mechanism allowing driven wheels to rotate at different speeds |
| Driveshaft | A shaft carrying torque to a wheel or axle module |
| Gear mesh | The contact and clearance relationship between mating teeth |
| Gear ratio | Relationship between input and output rotations |
| Pinion | Small driving gear commonly mounted on the motor |
| Spur gear | Larger driven gear commonly meshed with the pinion |
