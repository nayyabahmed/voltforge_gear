# Topic 3.10 - Suspension, Wheels and Grip

> **"A powerful buggy can accelerate, brake and steer only through the small tyre patches touching the ground."**

---

# Learning Objectives 🎯

By the end of this topic you will be able to:

- identify springs, dampers, arms, hubs, wheels and tyres
- distinguish suspension travel from ground clearance
- explain why a spring stores energy and a damper removes movement energy
- describe ride height, droop, compression and bottoming
- explain how tyres generate and lose grip
- predict basic forward, rearward and sideways weight transfer

# Before We Begin

Jump from a low step and land with straight legs.
Then imagine landing with bent knees.

Bent joints allow movement over time and reduce the sudden impact on your body.
A buggy's suspension similarly lets its wheels move relative to the chassis.

The goal is not to make the car soft and bouncy.
It is to keep the tyres following the surface while controlling chassis movement.

Every steering and motor command in Part 3 eventually depends on tyre grip.

---

# The Suspension Corner

One wheel module may contain:

- tyre and wheel
- hub or steering knuckle
- bearings
- suspension arm or links
- hinge pins or ball joints
- spring
- damper or shock absorber
- driveshaft where driven
- steering tie rod where steered

These parts form a moving geometry.

> **[Signature visual: one buggy corner shown at full droop, ride height and full compression. Wheel travel, ground clearance, spring compression and damper motion are labelled. Beside it, a bump trace compares spring-only bouncing with spring-and-damper settling.]**

---

# Springs Store and Return Energy

Compress a safe soft spring or foam pad and it pushes back.

A **spring** supports load and stores elastic energy when deflected.
For an ideal linear spring:

```text
Force = spring rate x deflection
F = kx
```

## Worked Example

A fictional spring rate is `0.8 N/mm` and compression is `5 mm`.

```text
F = 0.8 x 5
F = 4 N
```

The ideal spring force is `4 N`.

A stiffer spring needs more force for the same movement.
Preload changes the starting compression and ride height; it does not automatically change a linear spring's rate.

---

# Dampers Control Oscillation

A spring alone can return stored energy and make the chassis bounce repeatedly.

A **damper** resists motion and converts some movement energy into heat in fluid and seals.
It controls how quickly suspension moves and settles.

Too little damping can cause repeated bouncing.
Too much can stop the wheel responding quickly to bumps.

> **🤔 Think about it.**
>
> Press a ruler against a table edge, bend it gently and release it.
> It oscillates because stored elastic energy keeps changing form.
> What could remove some energy each cycle?

Friction or fluid resistance reduces the oscillation.
That is the damper's job.

Do not call the complete coil-over unit only a shock if you need to distinguish spring from damper.

---

# Travel, Ride Height and Ground Clearance

**Suspension travel** is the wheel's movement range relative to the chassis.

**Ride height** is the chassis position when the buggy rests under its normal load.

**Ground clearance** is the smallest important gap between the buggy and ground.

They are related but different.

**Droop** is wheel movement downward from ride height.
**Compression** is upward movement from ride height.

At maximum compression, a bump stop or chassis contact may prevent damage.
Reaching that limit harshly is **bottoming out**.
Reaching maximum droop harshly is topping out.

Check tyre, driveshaft, body and steering clearance at both extremes.

---

# Sprung and Unsprung Mass

The chassis, battery and most electronics form **sprung mass** supported by the suspension.

Wheels, tyres and some hub and arm mass move more directly with the road and form **unsprung mass**.

Lower unsprung mass can help the wheel follow rapid bumps.
Strength and durability still matter.

Do not weaken a hub merely to save a small mass.

---

# Wheels and Tyres

The wheel supports the tyre and connects it to the hub.
The tyre deforms where it touches the ground.

That small region is the **contact patch**.

Grip depends on:

- rubber compound
- tread pattern
- tyre temperature
- surface material and moisture
- normal load
- tyre deformation and insert
- wheel alignment
- slip amount

A larger-looking tyre does not automatically create proportionally more grip.
Compound and load behaviour are often more important.

---

# Friction and Grip

A simple dry-friction model says:

```text
maximum friction force ≈ coefficient of friction x normal force
```

```text
F ≈ μN
```

This is useful for first reasoning but real rubber tyres are more complex.
They deform, heat and interlock with surface texture.

Grip is shared between accelerating, braking and cornering.
Asking a tyre for maximum braking and maximum turning simultaneously can exceed what it can provide.

---

# Weight Transfer

When a bus brakes, passengers feel their bodies continue forward.
The vehicle load shifts toward the front tyres.

During acceleration, load transfers rearward.
During cornering, load transfers toward the outside tyres.

Weight transfer depends on:

- acceleration
- centre-of-mass height
- wheelbase or track width
- suspension behaviour

It changes how much grip each tyre can provide.
Lowering the battery can reduce weight transfer, but ground clearance and protection still matter.

---

# Camber and Alignment

**Camber** is wheel tilt viewed from the front.

- negative camber: top leans inward
- positive camber: top leans outward

Suspension movement can change camber.
A suitable curve helps the tyre use its contact patch while cornering.

Excessive camber reduces straight-line contact and can cause uneven wear.
Start with donor guidance and test.

Toe from Topic 3.9 also changes through travel if bump steer exists.

---

# Choosing Springs and Dampers

Setup depends on:

- vehicle mass and distribution
- motion ratio between wheel and shock
- terrain
- desired ride height
- available travel
- tyre grip
- driving speed

Change one thing at a time.

A spring change may require damping adjustment.
A tyre change can alter the best suspension setup.

Record the baseline before turning adjusters.

---

# Drop and Settle Tests

A useful low-energy test asks how the chassis settles after a small, repeatable release.

Observe:

- maximum compression
- number of oscillations
- return to ride height
- left/right symmetry
- contact or binding

Do not drop a powered buggy from damaging heights.

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Disconnect and remove the battery before suspension adjustment.
> Support the chassis securely before placing hands near moving arms.
> Springs and clips can eject; use eye protection and adult supervision for real shock work.
> Never open a pressurised or unfamiliar damper.
> Keep oils away from eyes and mouth and follow manufacturer disposal guidance.

# Hands-On Activity 1 - Spring-Only Bounce

Support a card platform on folded-paper springs.
Press and release from the same marked height.
Count oscillations.

# Hands-On Activity 2 - Add Damping

Let a card vane move through air between two close card walls or rub gently against a paper guide.
Repeat the release and compare settling.
Keep spring and release height unchanged.

# Hands-On Activity 3 - Grip Sled

Pull the same small card sled across paper, fabric and a tray with a little clean dry card texture.
Use the same mass and pull angle.
Compare the force using identical stretched-rubber-band marks rather than a formal measurement.

# Hands-On Activity 4 - Weight Transfer

Place four equal soft paper pads under a card chassis.
Move a coin model mass forward, rearward and sideways.
Compare pad compression.

# Hands-On Activity 5 - Clearance Envelope

Move a card wheel through droop, ride height, compression and full steering.
Trace the total swept area.
Keep body, wire and driveshaft models outside it.

# Topic Mini Project - Suspension Corner and Grip Tester 🛠️

Build a moving card wheel module with interchangeable paper springs, adjustable damping friction and a detachable grip sled.

## Materials

- corrugated card and scrap paper
- ruler, pens, scissors, tape and glue
- drinking straws or rolled-paper tubes
- rubber bands used gently, or folded-paper springs
- string
- coins used only as test masses

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Show a responsible adult what you plan to build and build with them nearby.
> Use age-appropriate scissors and keep rubber bands away from faces.
> Do not use a real battery, powered wheel, sharp shaft or pressurised damper.

> **🎬 Watch the build**
>
> - Science Buddies: search "rubber band suspension car" for simple moving-wheel ideas.
> - BBC Bitesize KS3 Physics: search "springs Hooke's law" and "friction".

## Step 1 - Make the chassis tower

Build a rigid card frame with a vertical scale.
Mark droop, ride height and compression.

## Step 2 - Make a moving wheel carrier

Use parallel card arms or a sliding straw guide.
The paper wheel must move vertically without twisting badly.

## Step 3 - Add interchangeable springs

Make soft, medium and stiff folded-paper springs.
Label each and keep their shape consistent.

## Step 4 - Add a damper slider

Use a paper strip through an adjustable friction guide.
Mark low, medium and high damping.

## Step 5 - Set ride height

Add the same coin mass for every setup.
Measure the resting wheel position.

## Step 6 - Run a settle test

Release the chassis from one marked height.
Record compression, oscillations and final position.

## Step 7 - Compare springs fairly

Change only the spring.
Keep mass, damping, release height and measurement method constant.

## Step 8 - Compare damping fairly

Use one spring and change only friction-guide setting.
Look for uncontrolled bounce and slow return.

## Step 9 - Add a swept-envelope card

Trace the wheel at droop, ride height and compression.
Add a full-steering outline if modelling a front corner.

## Step 10 - Build the grip sled

Attach a flat card tyre sample beneath a small sled.
Pull it with string over three surfaces using the same mass.

## Step 11 - Add fault cards

Create bottoming, topping, wheel rub, bent arm, loose pivot, too-soft spring, too-stiff spring, too-little damping and too-much damping.

## Step 12 - Diagnose and improve

Apply one fault at a time.
Change one variable and repeat the same test.

## Step 13 - Reflection

Record:

- best spring for the chosen load
- damping setting that settles without sticking
- ground-clearance and travel difference
- surface with greatest sled resistance
- limitations of card friction and paper damping

## Step 14 - Keep the artifact

Keep the tester, setup cards and results on your showcase shelf.
They complete the Part 3 RC systems collection.

# Thinking Like an Engineer - A Setup Is a System

A spring cannot be judged alone.
Its behaviour depends on mass, motion ratio, damper, tyre and terrain.

Write setup changes as evidence:

```text
Change: medium to stiff front spring
Question: does bottoming reduce?
Controls: battery, surface, speed and tyres
Result: bottoming reduced, steering grip also reduced
Decision: test medium spring with slightly more damping
```

This is engineering tuning rather than random adjustment.

# Test Plan

## Test 1 - Spring comparison

**Question:** Which paper spring supports the load without bottoming in the standard release?

**Independent variable:** Spring.

**Dependent variable:** maximum compression and ride height.

**Control variables:** mass, damping, release and structure.

**Procedure:** Run five releases per spring.

**Pass condition:** no bottoming and repeatable ride height within the model tolerance.

## Test 2 - Damping comparison

**Question:** Which setting settles the chassis most effectively without sticking?

**Independent variable:** Friction-guide setting.

**Dependent variable:** oscillation count and settle time.

**Control variables:** spring, mass and release height.

**Procedure:** Run five releases per setting.

**Pass condition:** quick return with no repeated bounce or delayed stuck position.

## Test 3 - Grip surface

**Question:** How does surface change sled resistance?

**Independent variable:** Surface.

**Dependent variable:** rubber-band stretch at movement start.

**Control variables:** sled, mass, pull angle and speed.

**Procedure:** Make five pulls per surface.

**Pass condition:** results cluster consistently enough to rank the surfaces, with limitations recorded.

# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌

## Mistake 1 - Calling a spring a damper

The spring stores energy; the damper controls motion and removes energy.

## Mistake 2 - Confusing ride height with travel

One is a resting position and the other is movement range.

## Mistake 3 - Adding preload to claim a stiffer spring

Preload changes starting compression, not an ideal linear rate.

## Mistake 4 - Making suspension extremely soft

Soft suspension can bottom and lose geometry control.

## Mistake 5 - Making damping extremely heavy

The wheel may fail to follow rapid bumps.

## Mistake 6 - Testing two changes at once

The cause of improvement becomes unknown.

## Mistake 7 - Ignoring clearance at full travel and steering

Ride-height clearance alone is insufficient.

## Mistake 8 - Assuming wider tyres always create more grip

Compound, surface, load and deformation also matter.

## Mistake 9 - Adjusting loaded suspension while powered

Remove the battery and support the chassis.

## Mistake 10 - Copying a racing setup without matching conditions

Mass, track, tyres and driver needs may differ.

# Topic Summary 📝

- Suspension lets wheels move relative to the chassis.
- Springs support load and store elastic energy.
- Dampers control oscillation and convert movement energy into heat.
- Ride height, ground clearance and travel describe different things.
- Droop and compression are the two sides of wheel travel.
- Sprung and unsprung mass respond differently to bumps.
- Tyre grip depends on compound, surface, load, temperature and deformation.
- Acceleration transfers load rearward, braking forward and cornering outward.
- Camber and toe change the contact patch and can change through travel.
- Fair setup tests alter one variable and preserve a baseline.
- Full-envelope clearance and serviceability come before speed.
