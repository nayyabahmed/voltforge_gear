# Topic 3.9 - Steering Systems

> **"Good steering does more than turn both wheels; it gives each wheel the angle its path needs."**

# Learning Objectives 🎯

By the end of this topic you will be able to identify steering parts, measure toe and throw, explain Ackermann geometry and build an adjustable rig.

# Before We Begin

Walk around a lamp post with one friend close to the post and one farther away.
The outside person travels a larger circle.

The same is true for a buggy's front tyres.
During a turn, the inside wheel follows a tighter circle and should normally turn through a greater angle.

# Steering Force and Motion Path

```text
servo -> horn/saver -> drag link or bellcrank -> tie rods -> steering arms -> knuckles -> wheels
```

Each joint changes direction, force, clearance or movement.

> **[Signature visual: top-down chassis turning left. Lines extended from both front wheel planes meet near the rear axle centre. Inside wheel angle is larger. Parallel-steer error is shown beside correct Ackermann.]**

# Steering Pivots

Each front wheel turns around a **steering axis** through its knuckle pivots.
The physical assembly may use kingpins, hinge pins or ball joints.

The tyre must clear suspension arms, body and chassis through steering and suspension travel.

# Tie Rods and Bellcranks

A **tie rod** transfers push and pull to a steering arm.
A **bellcrank** rotates about a chassis pivot and redirects movement.

Two bellcranks may form a steering rack with a servo saver.

Equal-length links do not guarantee equal wheel angles if pivot locations differ.

# Toe

Viewed from above:

- **toe-in** means fronts of the tyres point slightly toward each other
- **toe-out** means fronts point slightly away
- zero toe means wheel planes are parallel

Measure at wheel-centre height using the same tyre reference points.
Soft tyres and wheel wobble can confuse the result.

Toe affects straight-line stability, turn response, scrub and tyre wear.
Start with the donor or manufacturer recommendation.

# Steering Throw

**Steering throw** is the available steering movement from one side to the other.

More throw can tighten the turning circle until:

- tyre grip is exceeded
- geometry becomes poor
- a joint binds
- tyre hits chassis
- driveshaft reaches an unsafe angle
- servo reaches its load limit

Useful angle matters more than maximum angle.

# Ackermann Geometry

For ideal low-speed rolling, extended wheel directions meet at a common turning centre.
Because the inside wheel follows a smaller radius, it turns more.

This relationship is called **Ackermann steering geometry**.

Real RC cars use partial or adjustable Ackermann because tyre slip, speed and packaging change the best compromise.

Do not chase a perfect textbook point before the mechanism moves freely.

# Turning Radius

Wheelbase and steering angle influence turning radius.
For a simple bicycle model:

```text
turning radius ≈ wheelbase / tan(steering angle)
```

A `300 mm` wheelbase with a representative `30°` angle gives:

```text
radius ≈ 300 / tan(30°)
radius ≈ 520 mm
```

This simplified estimate ignores separate left/right angles, tyre slip and track width.

# Bump Steer

Suspension movement can change toe even when the servo does not move.
This is **bump steer**.

It occurs when tie-rod and suspension-arm arcs do not cooperate.

Measure toe at:

- full droop
- ride height
- compression

Small change may be acceptable.
Large change can make the buggy steer itself over bumps.

# Free Play and Symmetry

Check every joint while reversing direction gently.
Lost movement adds through the chain.

Compare left and right:

- maximum angle
- endpoint clearance
- tie-rod length
- tyre contact
- centring

Asymmetry may come from off-centre horn installation, unequal links or packaging interference.

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Disconnect the battery before adjusting links.
> Powered steering creates pinch points.
> During an adult-supervised servo test, keep drive power disabled and fingers away from horns and linkages.

# Hands-On Activity 1 - Circle Walk

Mark a centre and two walking paths with string.
Walk the same angle and compare inside and outside distances.

# Hands-On Activity 2 - Toe Measure

Use two card wheels and measure front and rear spacing at axle height.
Adjust one paper tie rod and classify toe-in, zero or toe-out.

# Hands-On Activity 3 - Ackermann Lines

Draw a top-view buggy.
Set two front wheel cards to different angles and extend their centre lines.
Adjust until they meet near the rear axle line.

# Hands-On Activity 4 - Bump-Steer Arc

Move a card knuckle through three suspension positions while holding a tie rod to a fixed chassis pivot.
Record toe-pointer change.

# Topic Mini Project - Adjustable Ackermann Steering Rig 🛠️

Build a top-down card chassis with two pivoting front wheels, adjustable tie rods and three Ackermann settings.

## Materials

- corrugated card, paper, string, ruler, protractor and pens
- scissors, tape and paper pivots

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Show a responsible adult what you plan to build and build with them nearby.
> Use age-appropriate scissors.
> Do not power the rig.

> **🎬 Watch the build**
>
> - BBC Bitesize KS3 Design and Technology: search "linkages mechanical systems".
> - Search a reputable RC manufacturer knowledge base for "Ackermann steering setup".

## Build steps

1. Draw wheelbase, track width and rear axle line on a card chassis.
2. Make two front knuckles with wheel cards.
3. Add steering-arm holes in three positions.
4. Make adjustable paper tie rods.
5. Add a central bellcrank or sliding rack.
6. Add a servo-input lever with conservative endpoints.
7. Set zero toe at centre.
8. Turn left and measure both wheel angles.
9. Extend wheel lines toward the rear axle.
10. Repeat for three steering-arm-hole positions.
11. Compare parallel steering, useful Ackermann and excessive difference.
12. Test right-turn symmetry.
13. Add a suspension-height slider and observe bump steer.
14. Add faults: unequal tie rods, loose pivot, horn off-centre, endpoint binding, tyre contact and excessive toe.
15. Choose the setting that moves freely and points closest to a common turning centre.
16. Reflect on why real tyres need track testing.
17. Keep the rig on your showcase shelf.

# Cheapest Valid Prototype

The card rig answers geometry and packaging questions for `£0` before a servo or knuckle is purchased.

# What to Buy, Reuse and Make

Reuse donor knuckles, bellcranks and links when sound.
Make chassis mounts, servo plate and geometry templates.
Buy precision ball joints, hinge pins and proven servo saver parts.

Record steering-axis positions, arm holes, tie-rod lengths, servo input, knuckle interfaces, wheel clearance, suspension positions and removal access.

# Test Plan

## Test 1 - Ackermann

**Question:** Which arm setting gives the most suitable inside/outside angle relationship?

**Independent variable:** Steering-arm hole.

**Dependent variable:** wheel angles and line intersection.

**Controls:** same chassis, toe and input.

**Procedure:** Measure three settings at equal input.

**Pass condition:** useful difference, free movement and no collision.

## Test 2 - Symmetry

**Question:** Are equal left and right commands similar?

**Independent variable:** turn direction.

**Dependent variable:** maximum angle and centre return.

**Controls:** same rig and endpoint.

**Procedure:** Repeat five turns each way.

**Pass condition:** results remain within chosen card-model tolerance.

## Test 3 - Bump steer

**Question:** How much does toe change with suspension position?

**Independent variable:** height position.

**Dependent variable:** toe change.

**Controls:** same steering centre and links.

**Procedure:** measure at droop, ride height and compression.

**Pass condition:** no binding and minimum practical toe change.

# Upgrade Path

Card rig -> donor geometry measurement -> unpowered rolling module -> drive-disabled servo test -> low-speed turning-circle test -> surface tuning.

# Stop Point Before the Next Purchase

Stop until wheelbase, track, knuckles, steering arms, links, servo input, toe target, wheel clearance and bump-steer plan are documented.

# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌

## Mistake 1 - Giving both wheels the same angle

The inside and outside tyres follow different turning radii.

## Mistake 2 - Measuring toe at different heights

Use matched reference points at wheel-centre height.

## Mistake 3 - Hiding unequal links with trim

Correct the mechanical geometry before electronic fine adjustment.

## Mistake 4 - Maximising throw until parts bind

Useful travel ends before hard contact.

## Mistake 5 - Ignoring suspension movement

Toe and driveshaft clearance must be checked through travel.

## Mistake 6 - Removing all joint clearance

Enough clearance is needed for free movement.

## Mistake 7 - Adjusting a powered linkage by hand

Disconnect the battery before mechanical adjustment.

## Mistake 8 - Changing several settings together

One-variable tests reveal which change caused the result.

# Topic Summary 📝

- Steering transfers servo movement to two knuckles.
- Toe describes wheel direction viewed from above.
- Steering throw is useful movement, not simply maximum angle.
- The inside wheel follows a tighter turning path.
- Ackermann geometry gives the inside wheel more angle.
- Bump steer is toe change caused by suspension movement.
- Free play, symmetry and clearance must be tested.
- Real track tuning follows a working, measured mechanism.

# New Words 📖

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ackermann geometry | Steering arrangement giving inside and outside wheels different turn angles |
| Bellcrank | Pivoting lever that redirects motion |
| Bump steer | Toe change caused by suspension movement |
| Steering axis | Line around which a wheel knuckle turns |
| Steering throw | Available steering movement across its range |
| Tie rod | Link carrying push and pull between steering parts |
| Toe-in | Front edges of paired wheels point slightly toward each other |
| Toe-out | Front edges point slightly away from each other |
