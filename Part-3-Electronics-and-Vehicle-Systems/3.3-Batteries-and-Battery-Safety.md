# Topic 3.3 - Batteries and Battery Safety

> **"Stored energy can bring a buggy to life.
> Good engineering decides when that energy must stay disconnected."**

---

# Learning Objectives 🎯

By the end of this topic you will be able to:

- Distinguish a cell from a battery pack and compare common battery chemistries.
- Read cell count, nominal voltage, capacity, energy and C-rating labels.
- Use four safety gates: inspect, identify, match and supervise.
- Explain why a fitting connector does not prove compatibility.
- Recognise the warning signs that mean stop, move away and tell an adult.
- Build and test a reusable battery safety station and passport.

---

# Before We Begin

The VoltForge buggy is waiting at the start line.
Its steering responds and its wheels are ready, but the battery is still disconnected.

On the bench are two packs with connectors that appear to fit.
One is correct; the other could exceed the electronic speed controller's voltage limit.
Can you identify it without plugging either one in?

That pause is real engineering.
A battery stores useful energy in a small space, but damage, a short circuit or incorrect charging can release it as intense heat and fire.

Your challenge is to decide whether one battery is **safe, suitable and ready** before energy enters the buggy.

We will make that decision through four gates:

1. **INSPECT** - Does the pack look and behave normal?
2. **IDENTIFY** - What chemistry and specification does its label state?
3. **MATCH** - Does every connected part accept those specifications?
4. **SUPERVISE** - Is the correct adult-led procedure and place ready?

Every activity uses paper cards or an empty cardboard model.
No real battery is charged, opened, modified or connected.

The four gates answer different questions.

| Gate | Question | Evidence | Stop example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inspect | Is its condition acceptable? | Body, wires, connectors and history | Swelling or crash damage |
| Identify | Do we know what it is? | Readable label and exact instructions | Missing chemistry or cell count |
| Match | Does the whole system accept it? | Documented ranges and correct polarity | Battery voltage exceeds ESC limit |
| Supervise | Is the adult-led procedure ready? | Correct charger, place and supervision | Charging will be left unattended |

An engineer does not turn an unknown into a guess.
It becomes a stop point until reliable information is found.

# The Non-Negotiable Boundary ⚠️

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Every real RC battery activity must be led and directly supervised by a responsible adult who has read the exact battery, charger and vehicle instructions.
> Never short, open, puncture, crush, bend, modify, dismantle or solder directly to a battery pack.
> Never charge or use a battery that is damaged, swollen, wet, leaking, unusually hot or behaving differently from normal.
> Charge only with a compatible charger and the chemistry, cell count and charging settings specified by the manufacturer.
> For LiPo charging, use the specified balance connection and a suitable fire-retardant charging container such as a LiPo safety bag, on a non-flammable surface away from combustible materials.
> A responsible adult must remain present and attentive throughout charging; never charge overnight or while leaving the building.
> If a pack becomes suspicious, do not touch it: stop, move away and tell the adult immediately.
> If there is smoke or flame, get out, stay out and call 999.

The exact product manuals take priority over general examples in this topic.
If the instructions are missing, damaged or unclear, the battery does not proceed.

# Gate 1 - Inspect the Pack

A pilot checks an aircraft before every flight because yesterday's safe landing does not prove today's take-off.
Battery inspection works the same way.

Look without squeezing, bending or opening the pack, using the same order every time:

1. body
2. wires
3. connectors
4. history

## Check the body

Stop for:

- swelling, puffing or a changed shape
- dents, crushing, bending or puncture
- split covering or exposed layers
- leaks, residue or unusual odour
- water exposure
- unexpected heat

## Check the wires

Stop for:

- cut, crushed or melted insulation
- visible conductor
- loose or pulled wires
- damage where a wire enters the pack or plug

## Check the connectors

Stop for:

- bent, recessed or exposed contacts
- cracked housings
- dirt, moisture or corrosion
- blackening, melting or heat discolouration
- unclear polarity

## Check the history

Ask:

- Was the pack dropped, crushed or involved in a crash?
- Did a charger show an error?
- Did the buggy stop through low-voltage protection?
- Was the pack left connected?
- Did it become unusually hot?
- Has its shape or performance changed?

Appearance is only one part of condition.
A pack can look normal while its history gives a reason to stop.

# Gate 2 - Identify What the Label Means

Two medicine bottles can share a shape while containing different medicines.
Battery cases and connectors can also hide different rules, so read the label and product instructions.

## Cell, battery and pack

One drinks carton is one container; several cartons in a carrier become one package.
A **cell** is one electrochemical unit that stores chemical energy.
A **battery pack** contains one or more cells connected and enclosed as a usable energy source.

People often call one cell a battery.
For RC specifications, the distinction matters because several cells can combine their voltage.

## Chemistry changes the rules

**Battery chemistry** means the materials and reactions used to store and release energy.

| Chemistry | Common use | Useful feature | Charging boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkaline | Household devices and some transmitter trays | Easy to obtain | Normally single-use; never recharge unless explicitly marked rechargeable |
| **NiMH** | Beginner RC packs and rechargeable transmitter packs | Familiar and relatively robust | Needs a compatible NiMH charger |
| **LiPo** | Many modern RC drive systems | High energy and current capability for its mass | Needs strict compatible charging, balancing, storage and damage procedures |

Connector shape does not identify chemistry.
Never select a charger programme from appearance or memory.

## Cell count and nominal voltage

Imagine stacking two identical step stools: the total height is the sum of both steps.
Cells connected **in series** similarly add their voltage differences.

The **cell count** label `2S` means two cells are connected in series.
A typical LiPo cell has a **nominal voltage** of `3.7 V`, a convenient named value used for classification.
Actual voltage changes with charge state and load.

```text
Pack nominal voltage = nominal voltage per cell × series cell count
```

## Worked Example - Reading a 3S LiPo

### The question

What nominal voltage should appear on a typical 3S LiPo label?

### Known values

```text
Nominal voltage per cell = 3.7 V
Series cell count = 3
```

### The rule

```text
Pack nominal voltage = voltage per cell × cell count
```

### The calculation

```text
Pack nominal voltage = 3.7 × 3
Pack nominal voltage = 11.1 V
```

### The meaning

The pack is described as a `3S 11.1 V nominal LiPo`.
This calculation does not choose charging, cut-off or storage settings; those come from the exact instructions.

## Capacity is not speed

Picture a small and a large water bottle feeding the same tap.
The larger bottle can supply water for longer, but it does not make the water leave faster.

Battery **capacity** is a labelled quantity of electrical charge, commonly stated in ampere-hours (`Ah`) or milliampere-hours (`mAh`).

```text
1000 mAh = 1 Ah
```

A `4000 mAh` pack has a labelled capacity of `4 Ah`.
Greater capacity may support longer run time, but it may also add mass, size, cost and charging time.

Real run time changes with terrain, gearing, acceleration, friction, temperature and battery condition.
Capacity is not a speed rating or a promise of exact run time.

## Energy combines capacity and voltage

One large suitcase may hold more than a small one, while lifting either suitcase higher also changes its energy.
Battery energy likewise depends on capacity and voltage.

A **watt-hour**, written `Wh`, is a unit of battery energy.

```text
Nominal energy in Wh = nominal voltage in V × capacity in Ah
```

## Worked Example - Comparing stored energy

### The question

What nominal energy is represented by a `7.4 V`, `4000 mAh` pack?

### Known values

```text
Nominal voltage = 7.4 V
Capacity = 4000 mAh = 4 Ah
```

### The rule

```text
Energy = voltage × capacity
```

### The calculation

```text
Energy = 7.4 × 4
Energy = 29.6 Wh
```

### The meaning

The nominal energy figure is `29.6 Wh`.
Usable energy and run time differ because the complete system has operating limits and losses.

## C-rating describes claimed discharge capability

A water tank may be large but have a narrow outlet.
Capacity describes charge quantity, while outlet size is more like current-delivery capability.

A **C-rating** is a manufacturer's claimed discharge-rate multiple of capacity.

For a fictional `4 Ah`, `25C` pack:

```text
Claimed discharge current = capacity in Ah × C-rating
Claimed discharge current = 4 × 25
Claimed discharge current = 100 A
```

This calculation explains the label; it is not permission to test the pack at `100 A`.
It does not prove voltage compatibility, cooling, condition, connector rating or real performance.

Use reputable parts, documented ratings and an adult-approved safety margin.
Never conduct a maximum-current battery test.

> **🤔 Think about it.**
>
> Two packs both say `4000 mAh`.
> One is `2S` and one is `3S`.
> Must they store the same nominal energy because their capacities match?

No. Capacity is only one part of the energy calculation; the `3S` pack has the higher nominal voltage, so it represents more watt-hours when both capacities are `4 Ah`.

# Gate 3 - Match the Whole System

A key sliding into a lock does not prove it belongs to that door.
It may turn the wrong mechanism or damage the lock.

A connector that fits proves only that two shapes can meet.
It does not prove chemistry, voltage, polarity or current compatibility.

The battery must match a chain of interfaces:

| Interface | What must agree |
|---|---|
| Battery and charger | Chemistry, cell count, voltage range, charging current and connection method |
| Battery and connector | Connector family, contact condition, current suitability and polarity |
| Battery and ESC | Chemistry support, voltage range, current demand and low-voltage protection |
| ESC and motor | Voltage, current, motor type and operating demand |
| Battery and chassis | Size, mass, protection, cooling, retention and removal access |

The electronic speed controller or **ESC** (the motor's speed controller, covered in Topic 3.6) may accept only stated cell counts and battery types.
Motor demand also changes with gearing and load, covered in Topic 3.7.

A pack can be healthy and still be wrong for the buggy.

## Main lead and balance lead

Many multi-cell LiPo packs have two connection systems.
The **main lead** carries drive current between the battery and ESC or a compatible charger.
The **balance lead** lets compatible charging equipment monitor individual cell groups.

**Balance charging** monitors and manages those groups within the equipment's permitted limits.

The balance lead is not a spare motor-power connection, and the main connector does not replace it.
Balance charging cannot repair a swollen, damaged or unsafe pack.
Repeated imbalance or a charger warning requires adult investigation.

## The match sheet

Record evidence before connection.

| Requirement | Battery says | Other equipment says | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chemistry |  | Charger:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |
| Cell count |  | Charger and ESC:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |
| Voltage |  | ESC and motor system:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |
| Charging current |  | Charger:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |
| Connector and polarity |  | ESC lead:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |
| Physical size and mass |  | Chassis allowance:  | PASS / STOP / UNKNOWN |

`UNKNOWN` is not almost-pass.
It means stop and find reliable documentation.

# Gate 4 - Supervise the Procedure

Think of the family kitchen.
Heat, sharp tools and stored energy are handled safely because the place, equipment and responsible person are prepared.
Charging is also a planned operation, not background housekeeping.

## Before charging

The responsible adult should:

1. read the exact pack and charger instructions
2. inspect the pack, leads and connectors
3. confirm chemistry and cell count
4. confirm the manufacturer-approved charging current and mode
5. choose a non-flammable surface away from combustible materials
6. keep the battery away from escape routes
7. use the specified balance connection and suitable LiPo charging container
8. check that the pack is cool and dry
9. agree the emergency route before charging starts

## During charging

The responsible adult should:

- remain present and attentive
- watch the charger display and battery condition
- prevent children or pets disturbing the setup
- stop for an error, swelling, unusual smell, hissing, smoke or unexpected heat
- never charge overnight, inside the buggy or while leaving the building

## After charging

The responsible adult should:

- end and disconnect the process exactly as the charger manual states
- inspect the pack again
- record unusual behaviour
- move the pack only when the instructions allow and it is safe to do so

> **🤔 Think about it.**
>
> Imagine two charging places.
> One is a carpeted hallway beside the front door.
> The other is an adult-approved non-flammable surface away from the escape route.
> Both use the correct charger.
> Are both plans equally safe?

No. Correct equipment is only one safety layer; the charging place, nearby materials, escape route and continuous adult attention also affect the plan.

## If something changes

The young engineer's response is simple:

```text
STOP → MOVE AWAY → WARN THE ADULT
```

Do not pick up, squeeze, smell closely or carry a suspicious battery.
Do not improvise a repair, disposal method or fire response.

If there is smoke or flame:

```text
GET OUT → STAY OUT → CALL 999
```

Do not attempt to move or extinguish a burning battery.
The responsible adult follows current fire-service, manufacturer and local-authority guidance.

## Retirement and recycling

A retired battery never goes into household rubbish or mixed recycling.
Damaged battery arrangements vary between councils and facilities.

The responsible adult should contact the council or approved recycling site before transporting a damaged pack.
The manufacturer may also give product-specific isolation and return instructions.

> **📚 Learn more**
>
> - [GOV.UK - Recycling batteries and electrical waste](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consumer-products-recycling-batteries-and-electrical-waste): the official UK route. Ask an adult to check it before any pack is moved, and to confirm damaged-pack arrangements with the local council.

# Storage, Transport and the Battery Passport

A buggy may run for minutes while its battery waits for days or weeks, so storage is part of the engineering system.

The **state of charge** estimates how much charge remains.
For LiPo storage, the responsible adult uses the manufacturer's storage procedure, never a guess from nominal voltage.

Store a pack:

- disconnected from the buggy and charger
- at the manufacturer-specified storage state
- cool, dry and away from direct sunlight or heat
- protected from crushing, puncture and falling
- away from loose metal objects that could bridge contacts
- in an adult-approved fire-resistant arrangement
- where children and pets cannot access it unsupervised

For transport, the adult protects each connector, prevents movement and checks current carrier or venue rules.
Airline and courier restrictions can change, so check the provider before travel.

## Battery mounting in the buggy

A suitcase needs both a lid and dividers.
A strap may stop a battery lifting out while still allowing it to slide into a sharp screw.
The tray and restraint must work together.

A safe mounting plan should:

- fit the pack without squeezing or bending it
- stop forward, rearward, sideways and upward movement
- keep sharp screw ends away
- separate wires from gears, shafts and hot parts
- allow required cooling
- permit quick inspection, disconnection and removal
- use a removable strap or cover
- survive the planned gentle retention test with a dummy pack

Do not glue a battery into the chassis.
Serviceability is a requirement.

## The battery passport

A battery cannot show its complete history on its casing.
A **battery passport** records one pack's identity, specification, use and condition.

Record:

- unique pack ID
- chemistry and cell count
- nominal voltage and capacity
- connector type
- purchase or first-use date
- inspections and charging events
- unusual heat, errors or performance changes
- crashes or physical damage
- storage checks
- retirement decision

The passport turns memory into evidence.

# Hands-On Activity 1 - Four-Gate Battery Detective

This activity needs no special equipment and no real battery.

## You will need

- scrap paper
- pencil

## Make four scenario cards

Write one scenario on each card:

1. A labelled pack has a swollen side.
2. An undamaged pack has no readable chemistry label.
3. A healthy 3S pack is offered to an ESC whose instructions allow only 2S.
4. A correct pack and charger will be left charging while everyone goes shopping.

## Steps

1. Read one card.
2. Name the first gate it fails.
3. State the evidence.
4. Choose `PASS`, `STOP` or `UNKNOWN`.
5. Explain what information or adult action would be needed next.
6. Swap cards with another learner and challenge their reasoning.

Pass when every unsafe or unknown scenario stops before connection.

# Hands-On Activity 2 - Decode the Fictional Label

## You will need

- paper
- pencil
- calculator if helpful

Draw this fictional label:

```text
VOLTFOX MODEL PACK - NOT REAL
LiPo | 2S | 7.4 V nominal | 4000 mAh | 25C
```

## Questions

1. What is the chemistry?
2. How many series cells are represented?
3. Convert the capacity to ampere-hours.
4. Calculate nominal watt-hours.
5. Calculate the label's claimed discharge-current figure.

## Expected calculations

```text
Capacity = 4000 ÷ 1000 = 4 Ah
Energy = 7.4 × 4 = 29.6 Wh
Claimed discharge current = 4 × 25 = 100 A
```

# Hands-On Activity 3 - Four-Direction Tray Test

## You will need

- one empty tea box or folded card battery dummy
- corrugated card
- paper strip or string
- tape
- ruler

## Steps

1. Measure the dummy's length, width and height.
2. Make a shallow tray with small side and end stops.

8. Confirm that the dummy remains easy to remove without pulling its paper wires.

Never replace the dummy with a real battery for this activity.

# Topic Mini Project - Battery Safety Station and Passport 🛠️

Build a keepable tabletop station that rehearses the complete battery decision.
A cardboard model pack moves through four gates while fault cards test whether danger is caught before connection.

## What the artifact teaches

The station embodies four habits:

- inspect condition
- identify specifications
- match interfaces
- supervise the procedure

It does not charge, test or store a real battery.
The responsible adult, correct equipment and exact manuals remain essential.

## Materials

- packaging card
- one empty tea box or folded-paper battery model
- scrap paper
- ruler
- pencil and coloured pens
- age-appropriate scissors
- tape or glue
- paper strip or string
- one reused envelope

No electrical equipment is required.

> **⚠️ SAFETY**
>
> Show a responsible adult or guardian what you plan to build before starting, and build with them nearby.
> Use age-appropriate scissors on a clear table and cut away from fingers.
> Do not use a craft knife.
> Use only an empty cardboard model marked `MODEL ONLY - NO STORED ENERGY`.
> Never place, charge, store or connect a real battery in this paper station.

```text
SMOKE OR FLAME: GET OUT → STAY OUT → CALL 999
```

## Step 7 - Make the passport

Fold a card booklet.

Add fields for identity, specifications, inspections, charging events, incidents, storage checks and retirement.

Give the model a unique pack ID.

## Step 8 - Make fault cards

Create at least eight cards:

- puffed side
- split covering
- exposed conductor
- recorded crash
- missing chemistry label
- wrong charger chemistry
- pack voltage above ESC limit
- unattended charging plan

Store them in the envelope.

## Step 9 - Run the gate test

Ask another person to hide one fault card beneath the model.

Move the model through the gates in order.

The inspector must find the first failed gate and send the model to `STOP`.

Repeat until all eight faults are caught.

## Step 10 - Reflect and keep it

Write three answers on the back:

1. Which gate prevents the most tempting guess?
2. Why can a healthy pack still be unsuitable?
3. What does your model station not make safe?

Keep the station and passport on your showcase shelf.
It is evidence that you learned to stop stored energy before it becomes uncontrolled.

# Thinking Like an Engineer - Safety Uses Layers

No single object creates battery safety.

A LiPo bag does not correct the wrong chemistry setting.

A correct charger does not repair crash damage.

An adult cannot supervise a process hidden behind a closed door.

Safety uses layers:

- suitable, reputable equipment
- clear identification
- condition inspection
- compatible ratings and polarity
- correct manufacturer procedure
- controlled charging place
- continuous adult attention
- mechanical protection
- records and retirement decisions
- a clear emergency route

If one layer fails, the process stops before stored energy is released.

# Test Plan

## Test 1 - Four-gate diagnosis

**Question:** Can the station stop unsafe or unknown battery scenarios?

**Independent variable:** Fault card presented.

**Dependent variable:** First failed gate and response selected.

**Control variables:** Same gate order, model pack and one fault per trial.

**Procedure:** Run all eight shuffled fault cards through the station.

**Pass condition:** Every unsafe or unknown scenario reaches `STOP` before the end.

## Test 2 - Label interpretation

**Question:** Can the learner extract and calculate the useful label information?

**Independent variable:** Fictional battery label.

**Dependent variable:** Correct chemistry, cell count, voltage, Ah, Wh and claimed-current interpretation.

**Control variables:** Same six answer fields and no real battery.

**Procedure:** Decode three fictional labels in a different order.

**Pass condition:** Every value has a unit, and missing information is marked `UNKNOWN` rather than guessed.

## Test 3 - Dummy-pack retention

**Question:** Does the tray restrain the battery dummy in every planned direction?

**Independent variable:** Tilt direction.

**Dependent variable:** Dummy movement and wire snag.

**Control variables:** Same dummy, tilt angle, tray and strap.

**Procedure:** Tilt gently forward, rearward, left and right.

**Pass condition:** The dummy stays retained, its paper wires remain clear and it can still be removed without pulling them.

# Common Beginner Mistakes ❌

## Mistake 1 - Calling a new pack safe

New describes its age, not its condition or compatibility.
Inspect every pack before every use and charge.

## Mistake 2 - Choosing by connector shape

A fitting plug does not identify chemistry, voltage, polarity or current suitability.

Use labels and exact documentation.

## Mistake 3 - Treating capacity as speed or maximum current

Capacity relates to charge quantity.

Voltage, C-rating and system demand answer different questions.

# Topic Summary 📝

- A cell is one electrochemical unit; a battery pack contains one or more connected cells.
- Chemistry determines charging, use, storage and damage procedures.
- A battery plan passes four gates: inspect, identify, match and supervise.
- `S` describes the number of series-connected cells.
- A typical LiPo cell is labelled `3.7 V nominal`, so 2S is `7.4 V nominal` and 3S is `11.1 V nominal`.
- Capacity in Ah or mAh describes charge quantity, not speed.
- Watt-hours combine nominal voltage and ampere-hour capacity to estimate stored energy.
- C-rating is a claimed discharge-rate multiple, not a complete guarantee of suitability.
- Main and balance leads perform different jobs.
- A fitting connector does not prove electrical compatibility.
- Real charging is adult-led, continuously attended and specific to the exact equipment instructions.
- A suspicious pack is not touched or tested; smoke or flame means get out, stay out and call 999.
- Storage, mechanical retention, records and responsible recycling are part of battery engineering.

# Answers 🔑
