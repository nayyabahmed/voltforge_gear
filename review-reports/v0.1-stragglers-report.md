# 2. What changed ✏️

## 2.1 Part 1 Review Quiz - Part D was asking readers to see something invisible

Part D is called "Can You Read a Failure?" and it is the section whose entire
job is recognition. Question 34 describes a fracture surface as *"smooth and
slightly shiny with visible lines running across it"* and asks what kind of
failure it is.

The quiz had one figure, in Part C. Part D had none - so the reader was being
asked to identify an appearance they had never been shown. Section 7 opens by
saying a reader learns what something looks like *by seeing one*.

Two placeholders were added:

- **Figure Q.2 (F1)** - two broken brackets side by side, one with a smooth
  glossy break and one rough and torn, deliberately unlabelled. Question 34
  now asks the reader to read the left-hand break *and* say how the right-hand
  one differs, which turns a recall question into a comparison.
- **Figure Q.3 (F5)** - the same bracket on day 1 and at week 3, sagging under
  an unchanged load beside a sunny window. Creep is hard to picture from a
  sentence and obvious from two panels.

Answer 34 was extended to cover the new half of the question: a smooth break
is usually a design mistake, a torn one is usually too much load.

## 2.2 Part 1 Review Quiz - two headings borrowed registered emoji

`# Part A - Do You Know the Words? 📖` used the New Words marker and
`# When You Are Ready for Part 2 ✅` used the Topic Checklist marker. Section 6
reserves both for template headings, and section 13.2 warns that the build
cannot distinguish a callout from a heading if the markers vary. Changed to
🗣️ and 🛫.

## 2.3 Topic 2.10 - 602 lines and not one external reference

The challenge asks the reader to design, print, cut, assemble and solder in a
single project, and sent them to no refresher for any of it - no Learn more
box, no Watch the build box, not even a search-form pointer.

Two boxes added: first-layer troubleshooting and manufacturing processes
beside the printing stage, and a soldering refresher beside the continuity-lead
stage, alongside the reader's own iron manual as the primary authority.

## 2.4 glossary.md - one word short of complete

`Brittleness` (Topic 2.6) was the single New Word missing from the glossary
book-wide. The glossary had `Brittle`; the topic bolds, teaches and lists the
noun form. Added as its own entry rather than renaming, because both forms
appear in prose, and `Brittle` gained the second line the house style uses.

**The glossary now covers every New Words table in all 41 topics** - 537
entries, no duplicates - which is what "Reviewed" should mean for this file.

# 3. Checked and deliberately left alone 🔍

Three things looked like defects and are not. Recorded so nobody "fixes" them.

| Thing | Why it stays |
| --- | --- |
| Topic 2.10 is 602 lines, below the 700-1,200 target | It is a challenge, not a teaching topic. The length lives in the reader's build, not on the page. Padding it to hit a line count would be exactly the wrong fix. |
| Topic 2.10 has only 3 of section 11's 8 build-topic elements | Correct. Section 11 scopes those to Parts 3-5. Its testing lives in "Stage 9 - Inspect, Test and Revise", where a builder actually meets it. |
| The quiz numbers its figures `Q.1`, `Q.2`, `Q.3` | A deliberate local convention. The quiz has no topic number, so `N.M.k` cannot apply. |
| The quiz has no think-about-it prompts | The whole document is a set of prompts. Adding one would be noise. |
| The quiz has no external references | It is assessment, not teaching. Its Revision Map points back at the topics, which is the right destination. |
